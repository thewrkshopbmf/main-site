Prayer Formation / Group Guide
For leaders · Six sessions · 45 minutes each

Lead men deeper than the request.

A six-session guide for men's groups, small groups and classes. Each session uses one foundation article, one passage, honest discussion and one practice for the week.

See the participant resources

Before your first meeting

  • Read the six articles yourself, including the full Bible passages around each one. Know what the text says before you lead a discussion about what it means.
  • Know your church's safeguarding process. Who is your safeguarding contact? Who do you go to if that person is the concern? Have both names before anyone shares anything.
  • Know the limits of confidentiality. You can't promise to keep everything secret. If someone is in danger, or a child or vulnerable adult is at risk, you'll need to act. Say this at the first meeting.
  • Make room for everyone. Ask privately whether anyone needs large print, a different format, or would rather not read or pray aloud.
  • Get your church leaders' review. This guide is a pilot. Have your pastor or elders review it before you use it, and follow your church's practices where they differ.

Read these ground rules aloud at every meeting

  1. You can always pass. On a question, a reading or a prayer. No explanation needed.
  2. What's shared here stays here, unless someone is in danger.
  3. We don't fix each other. We listen.
  4. Nobody here explains why someone else is suffering, or what God is saying to them.
  5. We ask before we pray over someone, lay hands on them, or follow up.

The 45-minute rhythm

  1. 5 min · Open. A check-in question. Anyone can pass.
  2. 10 min · Scripture. Read the passage aloud (volunteers only). Ask: what does it actually say? What doesn't it say?
  3. 20 min · Discuss. Start with the story or example, then move to personal reflection for anyone who wants to.
  4. 7 min · Practice. Do the exercise together, quietly, on paper.
  5. 3 min · Close. Pray. Short, silent or spoken. Name the practice for the week. Don't collect commitments.

The six sessions

Session 1 Prayer is a relationship, not a technique

Read before: What Is Biblical Prayer? · Matthew 6:5–15; Romans 8:15, 26; Hebrews 4:14–16

Big idea: Prayer isn't a technique for getting God to act. It's a son talking to his Father, through Jesus.

Open

Who taught you to pray, or didn't? What did prayer look like in the house you grew up in?

Scripture

Read Matthew 6:5–13. What two counterfeits does Jesus clear away before He teaches the prayer? Count the “I,” “me” and “my” in verses 9–13. (There are none.) Why might that matter?

Discuss

  1. Who are the “audiences” we pray for? The group, the pastor, our wives, ourselves?
  2. Where do we treat prayer like a technique we have to get right?
  3. Romans 8:15 gives us Jesus' own word, “Abba, Father.” What changes if prayer starts there?
  4. Paul says, “We do not know what we ought to pray for” (Romans 8:26). Why is that a relief?

Practice

Pray the Lord's Prayer together slowly, one line at a time, with ten seconds of silence after each line.

This week

Day 1 of the seven days: pray the Lord's Prayer for one other man.

Leader notes and safeguards
  • “Father” can be a painful word for men with absent or abusive fathers. Acknowledge it plainly. Don't push anyone to feel something about it. The article's line helps: Jesus isn't asking you to copy your dad onto God.
  • Don't ask the quietest man to pray aloud as an exercise. Invite. Never assign.
  • If someone says God must be disappointed in their prayers, ask what taught them that before you correct it.
Session 2 What to pray for: desire and motive

Read before: How Do I Know What to Pray For? · James 4:1–10; Philippians 4:4–9; John 15:7

Big idea: Ask. Then let Him reshape the asking.

Open

What's something small you'd never think to pray about?

Scripture

Read James 4:1–3. What's the setting? (A church that's fighting.) What two problems does James name, and which comes first? What does this passage not say about every unanswered prayer?

Discuss

  1. Two men want the same promotion. One wants to provide for his family. One wants to prove his old boss wrong. Most of us are a mix. How do you pray honestly with mixed motives?
  2. Why do men keep their prayers vague?
  3. Philippians 4:7 promises a guard on your heart, not a guaranteed outcome. Is that enough? Be honest.
  4. John 15:7 starts with “If you remain in me.” How does remaining change what you ask for?

Practice

On paper, privately: What do I want? Why do I want it (every reason)? What would Jesus want in this? Nobody shares unless they want to.

This week

Days 2 and 3: say what you want, then look underneath it.

Leader notes and safeguards
  • Never let the group vote on God's will for another man's decision.
  • If someone uses “God told me” to pressure someone else (a wife, a coworker), gently separate his conviction from the other person's freedom to decide.
  • A calm feeling isn't the same as a word from God. Big decisions need Scripture, facts and wise counsel, and sometimes a professional.
Session 3 After you pray: surrender and your part

Read before: What Do I Do After I Give It to God? · Mark 14:32–42; James 4:13–17; 1 Peter 5:6–8

Big idea: Surrender doesn't end your part. It ends your claim to run the outcome.

Open

What's something you've “given to God” more than once?

Scripture

Read Mark 14:32–42. Find three things: what Jesus says He wants, how many times He prays it, and what He does at the end. Then read James 4:13–17. What sits side by side in that paragraph?

Discuss

  1. Which lie do you lean toward: “If I let go, it all falls apart,” or “I gave it to God, so it's not mine anymore”?
  2. Use this example: a man is worried about his adult son. What's his to do? What's his to influence? What's his son's to decide? What's God's?
  3. Peter says, “Cast all your anxiety on him,” then, “Be alert.” How do you do both?
  4. Hebrews 5:7 says Jesus “was heard,” and He still went to the cross. What does that do to how you think about answered prayer?

Practice

Draw four columns on paper and sort one real burden. Circle one thing in the first column. Or use the Surrender Tool on your phone.

This week

Days 4 and 6: give it to God, then get up and do your part.

Leader notes and safeguards
  • Use a made-up example first. Don't ask men to map out someone else's marriage or family in front of the group.
  • Gethsemane is first the story of Jesus' unique mission. Never use “take up your cross” to tell someone to stay in an abusive or dangerous situation. Leaving danger, reporting harm and getting treatment can all be faithful steps.
  • If someone describes danger at home, pause the general discussion and offer a private conversation. Then follow your church's safeguarding process (see below).
Session 4 When God seems silent: lament

Read before: When God Seems Silent · Psalm 13; Psalm 88; 2 Corinthians 12:7–10; Mark 15:33–34

Big idea: Feeling forsaken isn't the same as being forsaken.

Open

No check-in question this week. Read Psalm 13 aloud, then sit in silence for a full minute.

Scripture

Compare how Psalm 13 and Psalm 88 end. Why would God keep a psalm with no happy ending in the Bible? In 2 Corinthians 12, what did Paul ask for, and what did he get? What doesn't the text tell us about the thorn?

Discuss

  1. When God seems silent, what do men usually do? (The article says: we go silent back.)
  2. What's the difference between what happened and what we concluded about God from it?
  3. Job's friends were great for seven days, then ruined it. What do we say to suffering men that we shouldn't?
  4. Jesus prayed “My God, my God, why…?” from the cross. What does it mean that He still said “my”?

Practice

Each man writes four lines starting with “How long…” privately. Then decide: end it like Psalm 13, or leave it open like Psalm 88. No one shares unless he wants to.

This week

Day 5: pray the hard question.

Leader notes and safeguards
  • This is the heaviest session. Let silence be silence. Don't rush to fix it, and don't require anyone to end on a hopeful note.
  • Never suggest that someone's suffering or unanswered prayer is caused by hidden sin or weak faith.
  • Don't compare: another man's healing or answer doesn't predict this man's.
  • Ask before you pray over someone or put a hand on his shoulder.
  • If someone talks about not wanting to be here, or about ending his life, take it seriously. Stay with him, ask directly whether he's thinking about suicide, and help him connect to 988 (call or text, U.S.) or 911 if he's in immediate danger. Then tell your pastor and follow your church's process.
Session 5 Waiting and who you are

Read before: When Waiting Begins to Define You · 1 Samuel 1:1–2:2; Romans 8:15–25; Psalm 27:13–14; Matthew 3:16–4:4

Big idea: You can wait for something without waiting to become someone.

Open

What's the longest you've ever waited for something?

Scripture

Read 1 Samuel 1:1–18. Find Elkanah's question in verse 8. What's wrong with it, even though he loves her? What changes in verse 18, and what hasn't changed yet?

Discuss

  1. Elkanah tried to fix Hannah's grief. Where do we do that to our wives, kids or friends?
  2. How does long waiting start to become a man's name?
  3. Jesus heard “This is my Son” before He'd done anything. Then the tempter said, “If you are the Son of God…” Where does waiting tempt you to prove who you are?
  4. Where did your timeline come from?

Practice

Privately, write: “I am waiting for ___.” Then: “I feel like less of a man because ___.” Is the second sentence true?

This week

Choose one faithful thing to do while you wait.

Leader notes and safeguards
  • Don't ask men to share fertility, marriage, health or job details. Let them name what they choose.
  • Hannah's story is not a formula. Don't promise anyone that his waiting will end the way he hopes.
  • Some losses are permanent. Grieving them is honest, not a lack of faith.
  • Ask the group what your church could change so men who are waiting don't feel invisible, then give that job to leaders, not to the men carrying the burden.
Session 6 Remain: prayer and life together

Read before: John 15:1–17; James 1:22–27; review the seven days

Big idea: Prayer isn't a project you finish. It's how branches stay connected to the vine, and to each other.

Open

What's one thing that's changed in how you pray over these six weeks?

Scripture

Read John 15:1–17. What does Jesus say about remaining? About asking? About loving each other? Then read James 1:22. How do hearing and doing fit together?

Discuss

  1. Where are you trying to produce fruit on your own?
  2. The Lord's Prayer says “our” and “us.” How could this group keep praying together after this series ends?
  3. What need has this group talked about but never acted on?
  4. What rhythm could help men come back to prayer without guilt when they drift?

Practice

Pray the Lord's Prayer together one last time. Then each man prays one sentence for the man on his right, silently or aloud.

This week

Start the seven days again with a brother.

Leader notes and safeguards
  • Offer care the group can actually keep up. Don't create rescue obligations.
  • Hand out the anonymous feedback questions below.

If someone tells you about harm

  1. Listen calmly. Thank him for telling you. Don't investigate, cross-examine, or ask the group to weigh in.
  2. Check safety now. If anyone is in immediate danger or there's a medical emergency, call 911. Prayer is never the only response.
  3. Be honest about confidentiality. Explain what you'll need to do next and who you'll need to tell.
  4. Follow your church's process. Go to your safeguarding contact, or the backup if that person is involved. Follow any reporting duties that apply to you. Don't tell anyone to confront an abuser.
  5. Keep it private. Share only with the people who need to act. This website has no reporting or monitoring service.

If someone is thinking about suicide: stay with him, ask directly, and connect him to 988 (call or text, U.S.) or 911 if he's in immediate danger.

After each meeting

  • Did we stay close to what the passage actually says?
  • Did every man feel free to pass? Did any advice turn into pressure?
  • Did we respect grief, silence and men's limits?
  • What practical help was asked for, and who has permission to follow up?
  • Does anything need to go to a pastor or someone more qualified?

Anonymous feedback (end of the series)

On paper, no names: Was the Bible teaching clear and faithful? Did you feel free to pass? Was the weekly practice realistic? Did the group respect grief and uncertainty? What should change? Tell the group who will read the responses and when they'll be thrown away.
