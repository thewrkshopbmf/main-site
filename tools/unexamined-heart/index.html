The unexamined heart.

“Behold, I thought…”

2 Kings 5:11 (KJV)

What am I defending, demanding, or concealing that may be keeping me from responding to God’s truth?

For leaders

Facilitator’s guide

Use this in men’s groups, mixed adult small groups, leadership teams and church discipleship settings. Invite participants to complete the reflection privately. Personal disclosure is always voluntary.

45-minute gathering

5 min connect · 10 min Scripture · 15 min small groups · 10 min prayer and response · 5 min send.

75-minute workshop

10 min connect · 15 min close reading · 20 min personal reflection · 20 min group reflection · 10 min response.

Four-session series

1. Expectations and surrender · 2. Desire and justification · 3. Integrity and correction · 4. Prayer and renewed obedience.

Four-session lesson plan

Session 1 · Behold, I Thought

Text: 2 Kings 5:1–14. Truth: my expectations are not automatically God’s instructions. Practice: facts-versus-story reflection.

Session 2 · When Desire Demands

Texts: James 1:13–15; 4:1–3; Genesis 4:3–8. Truth: legitimate desires never excuse sinful methods. Practice: the Agreement Finder.

Session 3 · The Hidden Pursuit

Texts: 2 Kings 5:20–27; Proverbs 16:2; Ephesians 4:25. Truth: proximity to ministry does not replace integrity. Practice: honesty and accountability audit.

Session 4 · Return and Respond

Texts: Hebrews 3:12–13; Psalm 139:23–24; Galatians 6:1–2. Truth: grace invites truthful repentance and new obedience. Practice: prayer and the seven-day path.

Rhythm for each session: read the passage in context; distinguish observation from inference; use one honest question; invite voluntary reflection; close with prayer and a concrete practice.

Small-group questions

  1. What did Naaman expect, and what did Elisha actually instruct him to do?
  2. Where is the difference between a legitimate desire and an entitled demand?
  3. How did Naaman’s servants help him reconsider his response? What makes correction safe to receive?
  4. What do Gehazi’s choices reveal about the danger of a respectable explanation for dishonest behavior?
  5. What is one specific response you want to practice this week, and what Scripture informs it?

Facilitator safeguards

  • Never require participants to share scores, written prayers, trauma histories or private details.
  • Do not interpret another person’s results as a spiritual diagnosis or determine whether they are saved.
  • Do not equate submission to God with obedience to an abusive or unaccountable leader.
  • In workplaces or mixed-belief settings, make religious participation opt-in and state the Christian foundation clearly.
  • Follow established safeguarding and reporting procedures if someone discloses abuse or risk of harm.
  • Do not collect identifiable participant responses without informed consent, security safeguards and a clear retention policy.
