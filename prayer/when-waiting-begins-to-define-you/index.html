When waiting begins to define you

You can wait for something without waiting to become someone.

The Revelation

At first, you're waiting for something.

Then, slowly, the waiting becomes who you are.

The guy who got laid off.
The guy whose marriage is “going through something.”
The guy whose son isn't speaking to him.
The guy still waiting on the test results.
The guy who's still single.

Nobody says it out loud.

But you can feel it in the room.

And worse, you can feel it in yourself.

The unanswered prayer has become your name.

Hannah

1 Samuel 1 opens on a woman who can't have children.

In her world, that wasn't just grief.

It was shame.

And her husband's other wife made sure she felt it:

This went on year after year. Whenever Hannah went up to the house of the LORD, her rival provoked her till she wept and would not eat.1 Samuel 1:7

Year after year.

Every trip to worship became a reminder of what she didn't have.

Then her husband tries to help:

“Hannah, why are you weeping? Why don't you eat? Why are you downhearted? Don't I mean more to you than ten sons?”1 Samuel 1:8

Men, read that slowly.

Elkanah loved her.

But he answered her grief with a question about himself.

He tried to fix it.

It didn't work.

You can love someone and still make their waiting about you.

Misjudged at the altar

So Hannah goes to the temple and prays.

Hard.

Lips moving, no sound.

The priest, Eli, watches her and decides she's drunk.

Even the religious leader got her wrong.

Her answer:

“…I am a woman who is deeply troubled. I have not been drinking wine or beer; I was pouring out my soul to the LORD.”1 Samuel 1:15

Poured out.

Not tidied up.

Not managed.

Poured.

The verse everybody skips

Eli blesses her. And then:

Then she went her way and ate something, and her face was no longer downcast.1 Samuel 1:18

Read the order carefully.

She isn't pregnant yet.

Nothing has changed.

But she has.

She poured out her soul to God, and something lifted before the answer came.

That isn't a formula. It doesn't mean pouring out your soul guarantees a baby, a job or a healing.

It means being heard by God can change you before your circumstances do.

Hannah's face changed before her situation did.

What she sang about

Samuel is born. She brings him back to the temple, just as she promised.

Then Hannah prays again:

“My heart rejoices in the LORD…”1 Samuel 2:1

Read her whole song in 1 Samuel 2.

It's about God. His holiness. His power. How He lifts the poor and humbles the proud.

She doesn't mention Samuel by name.

The thing she waited for wasn't the center of the song.

The God she waited on was.

And about a thousand years later, a young woman named Mary sang a song that sounds a lot like Hannah's (Luke 1:46–55).

Hannah's waiting became part of the story that led to Jesus.

The Lie

“I'll be okay when ___ happens.”

Which quietly means:

“Until then, I'm the guy who doesn't have ___.”

So life goes on hold.

Joy goes on hold.

Faithfulness goes on hold.

And you start waiting to become somebody.

The Truth

You can wait for something without waiting to become someone.

The longing is real. Don't pretend it isn't.

But it doesn't get to decide your name.

Already Sons, Still Waiting

Not only so, but we ourselves, who have the firstfruits of the Spirit, groan inwardly as we wait eagerly for our adoption to sonship, the redemption of our bodies.Romans 8:23

Here's what's strange.

Eight verses earlier, Paul says we've already received adoption (Romans 8:15).

Now he says we're waiting for it.

Both are true.

You're already a son.

And you're still waiting for everything that means.

Groaning is normal for sons.

You can be fully a son of God and still be waiting.

But if we hope for what we do not yet have, we wait for it patiently.Romans 8:25

One honest note:

What Romans 8 promises is the redemption of all things. Not every earthly hope.

Some things we pray for in this life won't come.

Some losses are real and permanent, and they deserve to be grieved.

But the final thing you're waiting for is certain.

Waiting Takes Strength

I remain confident of this: I will see the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living. Wait for the LORD; be strong and take heart and wait for the LORD.Psalm 27:13–14

“Wait” is in there twice.

And sitting right between them?

“Be strong and take heart.”

Waiting isn't passive.

It isn't sitting on the couch.

It takes courage to keep hoping when nothing moves.

And “in the land of the living” means here, now, in this life, not just someday.

David expected to see God's goodness while he was still waiting.

Look at Jesus

Jesus waited roughly thirty years before His public ministry began (Luke 3:23).

Thirty years, almost entirely unrecorded.

Then, at His baptism, before a single sermon or miracle:

And a voice from heaven said, “This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.”Matthew 3:17

Before He did anything.

Identity first.

Then, right away, the wilderness. And the first attack goes straight at that identity:

The tempter came to him and said, “If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become bread.”Matthew 4:3

“If you are.”

Prove it.

Hungry, alone, forty days in.

That's exactly what long waiting whispers to you:

“If God really loved you, you'd have it by now. Prove you matter.”

Jesus didn't take the bait.

He didn't need the bread to prove who He was.

The Father had already said it.

And because you're in Him, the Father says it over you too.

Jesus heard “This is my Son” before He did anything. Your identity doesn't wait on your answered prayer.

Upstream: Whose Timeline?

Where did your deadline come from?

Married by 30.
Kids by 35.
Promoted by 40.
Paid off by 50.
Healthy. Respected. Settled.

Some of those came from your parents.

Some from your friends' social media.

Some from church culture.

Hannah had Peninnah. You have your own.

The man with the life you wanted.

The comparison doesn't just hurt.

It tells you what to think about yourself.

Your pattern shapes how you wait, too:

The Prover waits by working harder, as if he can earn it.
The Controller waits by forcing it.
The Avoider waits by not thinking about it.
The Pleaser waits by keeping everyone else comfortable about it.
The Protector waits defensively, ready for any question.
The Reactor waits until it boils over.

Not sure which one is yours? Take the Pattern Finder →

The Audit

  • What am I waiting for?
  • What have I put on hold until it happens?
  • What would getting it prove about me?
  • Whose timeline am I measuring myself against?
  • What faithful thing is possible today, even while I wait?

Then:

Am I waiting for something, or waiting to become someone?

The Practice

Write two sentences.

“I am waiting for ______.”
“I feel like less of a man because ______.”

Look at the second sentence.

Is it true? Or is it something the waiting told you?

Bring both sentences to God, the way Hannah did. Pour them out.

Then choose one faithful thing for today.

Call a friend.
Serve someone.
Rest.
Ask for help.
Tell someone what you're waiting for.

Small counts.

And if it's a loss that won't come back, let yourself grieve it.

That's not a lack of faith. It's honest.

Take It With You

You can wait for something without waiting to become someone.

The Prayer

Father,

You know what I've been waiting for.
You know how long.
You know what it's done to me.

I pour it out to You: ______.

Don't let this waiting become my name.
You've already called me son.
Help me believe it before anything changes.

Give me strength to keep hoping.
Courage for today's faithful step.
And the honesty to grieve what I need to grieve.

Let me see Your goodness in the land of the living.
In Jesus' name,
Amen.

Hidden Gems 💎

You can wait for something without waiting to become someone.

You can love someone and still make their waiting about you.

Hannah's face changed before her situation did.

You can be fully a son of God and still be waiting.

Waiting isn't passive. It takes strength to keep hoping.

Jesus heard “This is my Son” before He did anything.
