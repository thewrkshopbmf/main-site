The Reactor · Anger and impulsivity

Why do I get angry so quickly?

A fast emotional surge can narrow the space between interpretation and response.

Start the 7-day practice Not sure this is you? Take the Pattern Finder
What it looks like

Responding impulsively to frustration or perceived disrespect

What may feel threatened

  • Feeling disrespected
  • Feeling unheard
  • A violated expectation
  • Fear of losing influence

Common first reactions

  • Raise my voice or sharpen my tone
  • Interrupt or accuse
  • Send a reactive message
  • Shut down abruptly

What often follows

  • Regret my words
  • Justify my reaction
  • Experience relational distance
  • Make amends
Where it shows up

Where this might show up

At workYou hear a disagreement as an insult and raise your voice.
In relationshipsA partner forgets something and you assume they do not care.
At homeYour child disobeys and you respond more harshly than intended.

Examples are illustrative, not proof of anyone’s motives. Responding firmly to real mistreatment can be appropriate.

Look beneath the reaction

What did I assume the other person intended?

Pause and ask: What happened, and what story did I immediately tell myself about it?

Practice a different response

Slow the moment, verify the meaning, and respond proportionately.

Pause, breathe, name the feeling, and ask one clarifying question before acting.
