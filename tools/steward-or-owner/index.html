The Steward vs. the Owner

Steward or owner?

“Unless the LORD builds the house, the builders labor in vain.”

Psalm 127:1

A private reflection for men carrying more than they were meant to. See where you’ve been living like an owner, and practice the shift back to faithful stewardship.

The teaching

Why control exhausts you

In the parable of the talents, the servants never owned the resources. They stewarded them. That changes everything. Stewardship says, “I’ll be faithful. I’ll obey. I’ll care well for what God entrusted.” But it also says, “I am not God.”

Many men confuse stewardship with control. So they micromanage, obsess, carry emotional weight constantly and lose their peace trying to secure outcomes. Some men are exhausted not because life is heavy, but because they’re trying to play a role only God can sustain.

Root check. Surface: “I feel overwhelmed. I can’t stop thinking about it.” Root: false sovereignty, fear-driven control, self-reliance, identity tied to outcomes. The core lie: “Everything ultimately depends on me.” The truth that breaks it: you were called to steward faithfully, not to carry the weight of being God.

For leaders

Use this with a men’s group

Invite men to complete the reflection privately before you meet. Don’t ask anyone to share answers. Teach from Matthew 25:14–30 and Proverbs 3:5–6, then let men share only what they choose.

A 45-minute gathering

  • 5 minWelcome. Read aloud: “Peace disappears the moment you start carrying what only God was meant to hold.”
  • 10 minTeaching: the servants never owned the talents (Matthew 25:14–30)
  • 20 minSmall-group discussion
  • 5 minEach man names one thing he’ll hand back to God this week
  • 5 minPrayer

Discussion prompts

  1. “Control is often fear disguised as responsibility.” Where do you see that in your own life?
  2. What’s the difference between responsibility and sovereignty?
  3. Why might a man carry everything himself if part of him expects God to make him pay for his past?
