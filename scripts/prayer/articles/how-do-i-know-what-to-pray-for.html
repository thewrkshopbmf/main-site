The Revelation

Some men don't pray because they don't know what to say.

Others don't pray because they think what they want is too small.

Or too selfish.

Or too specific.

So they keep it vague.

“Lord, just be with everybody.”

And never say what they actually want.

James has a word for that man.

James Names Two Problems

What causes fights and quarrels among you? Don't they come from your desires that battle within you? You desire but do not have, so you kill. You covet but you cannot get what you want, so you quarrel and fight. You do not have because you do not ask God. When you ask, you do not receive, because you ask with wrong motives, that you may spend what you get on your pleasures.James 4:1–3

Look at the setting first.

This isn't a lesson on prayer technique.

It's about a church that's fighting.

James traces the fighting back to desire: men who want something so badly they'll go to war with each other for it.

Then he names two problems in a row.

Problem one: “You do not have because you do not ask God.”
Problem two: “When you ask… you ask with wrong motives.”

Notice which one comes first.

Before wrong asking, James names not asking.

They wanted. They fought. They schemed.

They just never took it to God.

Some of us are fighting for what we never once asked God for.

And one more thing.

James is talking about a specific situation: desires driving a church to conflict.

He isn't saying every unanswered prayer means your motives were bad.

Don't let anyone use this verse to tell you that.

The Lie

“I shouldn't bother God with what I want.”

It sounds humble.

It's actually distance.

Because if you never tell God what you want, He never gets to reshape it.

You just keep wanting it on your own.

Every Situation

Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.Philippians 4:6–7

“Every situation.”

Not just the big ones.

The meeting. The money. The kid. The test result. The conversation you're dreading.

Now look at the promise.

Paul doesn't say: “and you'll get what you asked for.”

He says peace will guard your heart.

That's a military word.

Philippi was a Roman colony. The readers knew soldiers. They knew what it looked like to have a garrison standing watch.

That's the picture.

You hand over the request.

God posts a guard on your heart.

The promise isn't the outcome. It's a guard on your heart while you wait for it.

A Map, Not a Shopping List

When the disciples asked how to pray, Jesus didn't give them a list of approved requests.

He gave them an order:

“Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name, your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. Give us today our daily bread…”Matthew 6:9–11

His name.
His kingdom.
His will.
Then bread.

Bread is in there.

Your real, physical, everyday needs belong in prayer.

But they come after “Your kingdom come.”

Not because they don't matter.

Because they look different in that light.

Pray “Your kingdom come” first, and then ask for the promotion.

You'll notice you start asking differently.

The Truth

Ask. Then let Him reshape the asking.

You don't have to have pure motives before you pray.

Prayer is where motives get purified.

Bring the real request.

Let Him work on the man asking it.

The Promise Behind “Ask Whatever You Wish”

If you remain in me and my words remain in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you.John 15:7

People quote the second half.

Jesus started with the first half.

“If you remain in me and my words remain in you…”

This isn't a blank check.

It's what happens to a man whose life is rooted in Jesus.

His wishes change.

The longer His words live in you, the more you want what He wants.

And then you ask for it.

The more His words live in you, the more your asking sounds like His.

Look at Jesus

Here's something that changes how you pray.

Jesus has already prayed for you.

My prayer is not for them alone. I pray also for those who will believe in me through their message…John 17:20

The night before the cross, Jesus prayed for people who didn't exist yet.

That includes you.

And He hasn't stopped:

Therefore he is able to save completely those who come to God through him, because he always lives to intercede for them.Hebrews 7:25

Always.

Right now.

So when you don't know what to pray for, you aren't starting from nothing.

You're joining a prayer that's already going.

When you don't know what to pray, remember: Jesus is already praying for you.

Upstream: The Want Beneath the Want

Every request has something underneath it.

Take a promotion.

It might mean more money for your family.
A bigger chance to lead well.
Getting out of a toxic job.
Or finally proving your dad, your boss or your old coach wrong.

Usually it's a mix.

That's fine. Name it anyway.

Ask one question:

What would getting this prove about me?

If the answer is “that I'm finally enough,” you've found the real prayer.

And it isn't about the promotion.

Your pattern shapes your requests, too:

The Prover asks for success that proves his worth.
The Pleaser asks for everyone to be happy with him.
The Controller asks for certainty.
The Protector asks to be proven right.
The Avoider asks for the hard thing to go away.
The Reactor asks God to deal with the other guy.

A Word About Peace

Some men decide what God wants by how they feel.

“I have peace about it.”

Peace is a gift.

But a calm feeling isn't the same as a word from God.

Men have felt peaceful about terrible decisions.

And anxious about faithful ones.

Test it against Scripture. Get the facts. Ask wise men who'll tell you the truth.

And never use “God told me” to make a decision for somebody else.

The Audit

  • What do I want that I've never actually asked God for?
  • What am I fighting for that I've never prayed about?
  • What would getting this prove about me?
  • Am I willing to let God change what I want, not just whether I get it?
  • Who could help me see my motives honestly?

Then:

Am I asking God for it, or just wanting it near Him?

The Practice

Pick one thing you want.

Write down three answers:

What do I want? Say it plainly. No church words.
Why do I want it? Name every reason, even the ugly ones.
What would Jesus want in this? Not just the outcome. The man you become getting there.

Then pray it with “Your kingdom come” in front of it.

And see what changes.

Your name first.

Purify what's selfish.
Strengthen what's good.
Change the man who's asking.

Thank You that Jesus is already praying for me.
Let my asking sound more like His.

In Jesus' name,
Amen.
