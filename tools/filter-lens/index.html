The Filter Lens · Movement One: Recognize

What’s been shaping the way you see?

“Search me, God, and know my heart.”

Psalm 139:23

Ten questions to notice what has been shaping how you see, believe, desire and respond. Not a test of your worth. An invitation to honest reflection before God.

Share Text Email
The teaching

The filter you never examined

No man sees life neutrally. Family, culture, wounds, wins and habits all shape the lens you look through, usually before you know you’re learning anything. Some of what you absorbed is wise. Some of it needs a second look.

Jeremiah 17:9–10 warns that the heart can deceive us, and points to the Lord who searches it. Romans 12:2 calls us to be transformed by the renewing of the mind. And in 2 Corinthians 10:3–5, Paul talks about taking every thought captive to obey Christ. None of that is a call to obsess over every passing thought. It’s an invitation to humble, honest examination, with the One who already knows you fully.

Discard, submit, embrace. As you notice your filters, some things need to go, some need to be handed to God, and some are good and worth holding onto.

For leaders

Use this with a men’s group

Ask men to work through a few questions privately before you meet, or take one question a week. Don’t ask anyone to share what they wrote. Teach from Psalm 139:23–24 and Romans 12:2, then let men share what they choose.

A 45-minute gathering

  • 5 minWelcome. Read aloud: “A belief can be familiar without being true.”
  • 10 minTeaching: Psalm 139:23–24 and Romans 12:2
  • 20 minSmall groups: talk through one question together
  • 5 minEach man names one thing to discard, submit or embrace this week
  • 5 minPray Psalm 139:23–24 over each other

Discussion prompts

  1. Who taught you to see life the way you see it? What did they get right?
  2. “Sometimes the filter is so familiar we mistake it for our personality.” Where do you see that in yourself?
  3. What’s the difference between examining your heart and being suspicious of yourself?
