What Do You Have?

What’s in your hand?

“Then the LORD said to him, ‘What is that in your hand?’ ‘A staff,’ he replied.”

Exodus 4:2

A private inventory for men who feel they don’t have enough to offer. See what God has already put in your hands, and take one step to put it to work.

Versions

Send the version that fits

The inventory adjusts its prompts, Scripture, seven-day path and help line for where a man is. Each version has its own link:

  • After a loss: job loss, divorce or retirement. “What do you still have when everything is taken?”
  • Veterans & soldiers: what God trained into you in uniform.
  • Young men: you’re not too young for God to use what’s in your hand.
  • Couples: “What’s in your house?” An inventory to take together.
The pattern in Scripture

Twelve times God started with what was already there

All through the Bible, God asks some version of the same question. Not because He lacks information, but because the question exposes what people trust, what they overlook, what they’re withholding and what He intends to multiply.

MosesExodus 4:2
“What is that in your hand?” An ordinary shepherd’s staff parted the sea.
The widow and her sons2 Kings 4:2
“What do you have in your house?” A small jar of oil filled every jar she could borrow.
The boy with the lunchJohn 6:9
“Here is a boy with five small barley loaves and two small fish.” Thousands ate, with twelve baskets left over.
David1 Samuel 17:40
A sling and five smooth stones He used what he’d practiced in the fields, not Saul’s armor.
GideonJudges 6:14
“Go in the strength you have.” God sent him out of weakness, with His presence as the guarantee.
The widow of Zarephath1 Kings 17:12
“Only a handful of flour in a jar and a little olive oil in a jug” She gave from the last of it, and it didn’t run out.
Peter at the temple gateActs 3:6
“What I do have I give you.” No silver or gold, but a man walked for the first time.
The woman with the alabaster jarMark 14:3
“What are you holding back?” She poured it all out, and Jesus said it would be remembered.
The rich young rulerMark 10:21–22
“What are you unwilling to release?” He walked away sad, holding on to what he couldn’t keep.
The servants and the talentsMatthew 25:14–30
“What did you do with what you were given?” The one who buried his talent out of fear lost it.
The poor widowMark 12:41–44
“Out of her poverty, put in everything—all she had to live on” Jesus said she gave more than all the others.
MaryLuke 1:38
“I am the Lord’s servant.” Sometimes all you have is a yes, and a yes is enough.

The human pattern says, “I don’t have enough.” God’s pattern asks, “What do you have?” The miracle is often hidden inside the thing people dismissed.

For leaders

Use this with a men’s group

Looking for your next leaders? The Spotting Leaders guide shows how to use this inventory in intake calls and groups to see which men God is raising up.

Invite men to take the inventory before you meet. Don’t ask anyone to share answers they don’t want to. Teach from Exodus 4:1–17 and John 6:5–13, then let men share what they choose.

A 45-minute gathering

  • 5 minWelcome and the question: “What’s in your hand?”
  • 10 minTeaching: Moses’ staff and the boy’s lunch
  • 20 minSmall-group discussion
  • 5 minEach man names one step he’ll take with what he has
  • 5 minPrayer with open hands

Discussion prompts

  1. Which excuse do you reach for most: too small, not qualified, too young or old, not enough, my past, too weak?
  2. What has God trained you in quietly, like David in the fields, that you’ve never thought of as useful?
  3. “What you keep shrinks. What you pour out multiplies.” Where have you seen that be true?
