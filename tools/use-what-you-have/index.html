Resources / Use What You Have
For leaders, managers and coaches

Use what you have.

A private 10-minute inventory for leaders who keep waiting on more budget, more people or more authority. Find what’s already in your hands, and put one thing to work this week.

For teams

Workshop: “What’s already in this room?”

A 45-minute session for a team meeting or offsite. Ask everyone to take the inventory privately first. Nobody has to share their answers.

  • 5 minName the constraint everyone keeps mentioning: budget, headcount, time, tools.
  • 10 minEach person names one asset they bring that the team underuses.
  • 10 minEach person names one asset they see in a teammate. Go around the room so everyone hears one.
  • 15 min“What could we do with only what we already have?” Pick one idea to try in the next two weeks.
  • 5 minEach person commits to one step out loud. Set a date to report back.

Ground rules: no idea needs more budget to be discussed; praise is specific (“When you ___, it helped ___”); what’s shared in the room stays in the room.

For coaches

A first coaching session built on the inventory

Send the link before your first session. Then use these questions in order:

  1. Walk me through what’s already in your hands. What surprised you as you wrote it down?
  2. Which one have you been calling “not enough”? What do you usually tell yourself about it?
  3. What are you holding that would multiply if you gave it away: credit, information, access, a skill?
  4. Who needs what you have right now?
  5. What one step will you take before we meet again? How will I know you did it?

Close by asking what support they need from you, and schedule the next conversation before you hang up.

Where this comes from

A principle as old as Moses’ staff

This inventory is built on a pattern that runs through the Bible. When God called Moses, Moses listed every reason he wasn’t enough. God asked one question: “What is that in your hand?” It was an ordinary staff. A widow with a little oil, a boy with five loaves and two fish, a shepherd with a sling: each time, God started with what was already there. For the faith-based version, take What’s in Your Hand?
