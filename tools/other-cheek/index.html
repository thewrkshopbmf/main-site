The other cheek: what Jesus actually meant

“If anyone slaps you on the right cheek, turn to them the other cheek also.”

Matthew 5:39

Not “be a doormat.” Not “impossible ideal.” Not a weapon for someone who wants to keep hurting you. A private, interactive study of one of the most misused things Jesus ever said.

Share Text Email
The teaching

Who governs your response?

Jesus spoke to people living under Roman occupation, in an honor-and-shame culture where a slap to the face was about humiliation more than pain. Matthew says the right cheek, which may point to a backhanded insult, though that detail is debated. Either way, the question isn’t only, “What do you do when someone hits you?” It’s, “Who governs your response when someone treats you unjustly?”

The words. “Do not resist” translates antistēnai, to stand against or oppose. In context, with a slap, a lawsuit, a forced mile and a demand, Jesus is addressing how His followers respond to people who wrong them: don’t repay injury with injury. And “an eye for an eye” was a rule for courts that limited punishment, not a license for personal revenge.

What it isn’t. Jesus Himself pushed back with words when He was struck (John 18:23), stayed silent before false witnesses, walked away from a mob and stopped Peter’s sword. Scripture calls us to resist the devil, defend the weak and submit to authorities who punish wrongdoing. Refusing revenge doesn’t mean refusing truth, boundaries, justice or protection.

What it is. A radical, active refusal to let evil set your terms. “Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you” (Matthew 5:44). The other person may start the offense, but he doesn’t have to become the author of your response.

For more on handling offense, see Sharpened, Not Sheltered and How do I control my anger as a Christian man?

For leaders

A five-week series for men’s groups

Have men take this once before the series begins. Then take one week for each part. Some men in your group may have had this verse used against them in an abusive situation. Go gently, never press for details, and make the safety line clear on the first night.

  • Week 1What Jesus actually said. Read Matthew 5:38–48 whole. Context, the Greek words and “an eye for an eye.”
  • Week 2How Jesus responded when He was wronged: speaking, silence, leaving, the sword, the cross (John 18, Matthew 26, Luke 4, 1 Peter 2).
  • Week 3The cheek, the cloak, the mile and the one who asks. Voluntary sacrifice versus coerced exploitation.
  • Week 4Forgiveness, repentance, reconciliation and trust: six things we collapse into one (Luke 17:3–4, Romans 12:18).
  • Week 5When justice becomes vengeance: Cain, Simeon and Levi, David and Saul, Absalom, James and John (Romans 12:17–21).

Discussion prompts

  1. How have you heard “turn the other cheek” used, or misused?
  2. Which of Jesus’ five responses is hardest for you: speaking up, staying silent, walking away, putting the sword down or enduring?
  3. What’s the difference between wanting justice and wanting him to pay?
