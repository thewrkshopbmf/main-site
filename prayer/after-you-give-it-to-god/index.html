What do I do after I give it to God?

Surrender doesn't end your part. It ends your claim to run the outcome.

The Revelation

Everybody says it.

“Just give it to God.”

Good advice.

Until 2 a.m.

When you pick it back up.

So what does giving it to God actually look like?

Go to the garden.

The night before the cross, Jesus takes Peter, James and John into Gethsemane. And He prays:

“Abba, Father,” he said, “everything is possible for you. Take this cup from me. Yet not what I will, but what you will.”Mark 14:36

Mark records three things most of us read right past.

1. He said what He wanted.

“Take this cup from me.”

Jesus didn't pretend He wanted nothing.

He named the desire out loud. Then He yielded it.

Surrender doesn't start with pretending. It starts with honesty.

2. He prayed it again.

Once more he went away and prayed the same thing.Mark 14:39

Matthew says He did it three times (Matthew 26:44).

The Son of God came back to the same burden.

Three times.

So when you bring it back to God again tonight, that isn't proof your surrender failed.

Coming back to the same prayer isn't failing. Jesus did it.

3. Then He got up.

“Rise! Let us go! Here comes my betrayer!”Mark 14:42

He didn't surrender and stay on the ground.

He surrendered and walked straight toward the hardest thing in front of Him.

Surrender didn't take Jesus out of the garden. It sent Him into the night.

Luke adds two details.

An angel from heaven appeared to him and strengthened him. And being in anguish, he prayed more earnestly…Luke 22:43–44

Strength came.

The anguish didn't leave.

God's help didn't end the struggle. It carried Him through it.

Some of the earliest manuscripts of Luke don't include verses 43–44, and many Bibles note that. Nothing in this article depends on those verses alone. Mark tells the same story of honest anguish and surrender.

And the disciples?

…he found them asleep, exhausted from sorrow.Luke 22:45

They weren't lazy. They were overwhelmed.

Some of us don't take the burden back.

We go numb.

Sleep, scroll, stay busy. That's its own way of not carrying it.

Jesus' word to them is His word to us:

“Get up and pray so that you will not fall into temptation.”Luke 22:46

The Lie

Two lies sit on opposite sides of “give it to God.”

Lie #1: “If I let go, it all falls apart.”

So you keep checking.
Rehearsing.
Managing.
Texting.
Fixing.

Lie #2: “I gave it to God, so it's not mine anymore.”

So you skip the hard conversation.
The appointment.
The apology.
The bill.
And call it faith.

One grabs control.

The other drops responsibility.

Both sound spiritual.

James Saw Both

Now listen, you who say, “Today or tomorrow we will go to this or that city, spend a year there, carry on business and make money.” Why, you do not even know what will happen tomorrow. What is your life? You are a mist that appears for a little while and then vanishes. Instead, you ought to say, “If it is the Lord's will, we will live and do this or that.”James 4:13–15

James isn't against planning.

Look at what these men say:

Today or tomorrow.
This city.
A year.
Business.
Money.

Every detail is theirs.

God isn't in the sentence.

Then, two verses later:

If anyone, then, knows the good they ought to do and doesn't do it, it is sin for them.James 4:17

Same paragraph.

The man who presumes he owns tomorrow.
The man who knows the good and doesn't do it.

James puts them side by side.

Surrender has two enemies: grabbing what's God's and dropping what's yours.

What “Cast It” Actually Means

“Give it to God” mostly comes from here:

Humble yourselves, therefore, under God's mighty hand, that he may lift you up in due time. Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you. Be alert and of sober mind.1 Peter 5:6–8

Look closer.

In Greek, “cast” isn't a brand-new command.

It hangs on “humble yourselves.”

Read it that way: humble yourselves… by casting all your anxiety on Him.

Casting your worry is how you humble yourself.

Which means holding it is a quiet way of saying:

“I'm the one who has to make this work.”

Now look at the very next line:

“Be alert and of sober mind.”

Peter doesn't say: cast it and check out.

He says: cast it and stay awake.

Giving it to God isn't checking out. It's staying awake without carrying what isn't yours.

The Truth

Surrender doesn't end your part. It ends your claim to run the outcome.

You still show up.
You still make the call.
You still tell the truth.
You still do the next right thing.

But you stop needing the result to prove you did enough.

And sometimes your part is asking for help.

If you or someone else isn't safe, getting help isn't a lack of faith.

It is your part.

Look at Jesus

Gethsemane isn't mainly a lesson in how to surrender.

It's where Jesus surrendered for you.

During the days of Jesus' life on earth, he offered up prayers and petitions with fervent cries and tears to the one who could save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverent submission.Hebrews 5:7

Read that slowly.

He was heard.

And He still went to the cross.

“Heard” didn't mean the cup was taken away.

The Father answered on the other side of death.

Sunday morning.

The surrender that began in the garden finished on the cross:

“Father, into your hands I commit my spirit.”Luke 23:46

Here's the gospel in the garden:

Jesus said “not my will” when it cost Him everything.

So you can say it when it costs you something, and find a Father on the other side who can be trusted.

You can release the outcome because Jesus released His life into the Father's hands, and the Father raised Him.

Upstream: Why We Take It Back

You don't take it back because you're weak.

You take it back because of something you believe.

Watch the progression:

  1. Something happens
  2. I tell myself what it means
  3. Fear gets loud
  4. I grab the wheel, or I go numb

Your son hasn't called back in a week.

“He's pulling away.”

Fear: I'm losing him.

So it's five texts and a lecture you rehearse in your head.

Or you stop reaching out, so it can't hurt.

Your pattern shapes how you take it back:

The Controller tightens the grip.
The Protector defends every call he made about it.
The Pleaser carries everybody else's feelings about it.
The Avoider sets down his part along with the outcome.
The Prover works harder, as if he could earn the result.
The Reactor lets frustration make the next move.

The Four Columns

Take the thing you keep picking back up.

Sort it.

Mine to doWhat I can actually do. Honest, safe, within my capacity.
Mine to influenceI can ask, invite, advise. I can't make.
Theirs to decideAnother person's choices belong to them.
God's to determineTiming. Outcomes. Tomorrow.

Do your part. Offer your influence. Respect their choice. Trust God with the rest.

The Audit

  • What do I keep picking back up?
  • What have I called “trusting God” that is really avoiding my part?
  • What have I called “being responsible” that is really trying to control another person?
  • What am I afraid will happen if I stop checking?
  • What one thing in column one have I been putting off?

Then:

Am I carrying this, or is it carrying me?

The Surrender

Pray it the way Jesus did.

Name it. Then yield it.

“Father, this is what I want: ______. Yet not what I will, but what You will.”

Then get up.

Do the one thing in column one.

Today.

And when you pick it back up tonight?

Go back. Pray the same thing.

He did.

Take It With You

The Prayer

Father,

You know what I want.
I'm not going to pretend I don't.
Here it is: ______.

Forgive me where I've grabbed what's Yours.
Where I've dropped what's mine.
Where I've called fear “responsibility”
and called avoidance “faith.”

Give me courage for my part.
Wisdom to respect what belongs to others.
Trust for what only You can do.

Not my will, but Yours be done.

And when I pick it back up,
teach me to bring it back to You again.

In Jesus' name,
Amen.

  • Casting your worry is how you humble yourself.
  • Give it to God, and stay awake.
