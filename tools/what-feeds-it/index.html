The Anatomy of Lust

What’s feeding it?

“The eye is the lamp of the body. If your eyes are healthy, your whole body will be full of light.”

Matthew 6:22

A private self-check for men who are tired of losing the same fight. Find the roots that may be feeding lust, the moments it tends to hit, and where to start getting free.

The full anatomy

Twenty-five roots that feed lust

Lust rarely has just one cause. These are the root systems behind it, grouped in the self-check into six. Some, like the flesh and the culture around us, are in every man. Others are specific to your story.

  1. Biological desire
  2. The flesh (fallen human nature)
  3. The eyes
  4. An undisciplined imagination
  5. Dopamine conditioning
  6. Loneliness
  7. Emotional pain
  8. Stress relief
  9. Boredom
  10. Lack of mission
  11. Identity problems
  12. Pride
  13. Entitlement
  14. Lack of self-control
  15. Spiritual drift
  16. Neglecting the heart
  17. Cultural saturation
  18. Pornography
  19. Comparison
  20. Unresolved childhood wounds
  21. Poor boundaries
  22. Lack of brotherhood
  23. Spiritual warfare
  24. Forgetting the cost
  25. Misunderstanding love

Jesus put the eye in the middle of Matthew 6 on purpose: treasure (where your heart goes), the eye (what you keep looking at), then masters (who you end up serving). What you look at shapes what you treasure, and what you treasure becomes your master.

For leaders

Use this with a men’s group

Invite men to take the self-check privately before you meet. Never ask anyone to reveal answers or results. Teach from Matthew 6:19–24 and 2 Samuel 11:1–4, then let men share only what they choose.

A 45-minute gathering

  • 5 minWelcome and a reminder that this room is safe
  • 10 minTeaching: treasure, the eye and masters (Matthew 6:19–24)
  • 20 minSmall-group discussion in groups of three or four
  • 5 minOne guardrail each man will put in place this week
  • 5 minPrayer

Discussion prompts

  1. Where did David’s fall really begin in 2 Samuel 11?
  2. “Many men aren’t addicted to pleasure. They’re starving for something.” What do you think men are starving for?
  3. Why does shame push us to hide, and what changes when a man brings it into the light?
